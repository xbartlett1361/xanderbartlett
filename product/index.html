Case studyRemira T1D, product

Remira T1D · Co-founder & CEO, acting PM · 2026 to now

Built to lighten the load, not add to it.

Remira reads a Dexcom CGM and turns raw glucose data into a forecast of the patterns you'll face today, the few decisions that matter most, and a daily recap of what worked. I co-founded it and ran product with a six-person cross-functional team. The lesson: the product was never my vision alone. It came from listening to users, borrowing from giant consumer apps, and iterating.

02 · The process

Double Diamond: widen, narrow, twice.

Discover

Ask what they do, not what they want.

24 interviews with patients, doctors, a former competitor, activists and creators. I lived in the T1D TikTok feed and tore down Duolingo, Flo and Strava.

Define

Impact over effort.

Cut discussion forums (low impact, high effort), Google Calendar sync (low impact), and sponsored spotlights (high effort). What was left became one core thesis.

Develop

Wireframe, then invent.

Five 30-page wireframe docs turned complex mechanics into dead-simple screens. Out of them came the Reinforcing Cycle and Decision Zones.

Deliver

Let users choose.

Three paths, ranked by beta testers. I finished the winner with a UI designer, then walked the dev team through the high-fidelity design.

04 · Invention

The Reinforcing Cycle.

Most diabetes apps are dashboards: they show you numbers and leave the work to you. Remira is a loop. You can enter at any point, every point moves you to the next, and moving through it feels good, so you want to keep going.

Reflect

Yesterday, wrapped.

Every 24 hours: what happened, one good thing, and whether yesterday's decision zones worked.

Predict

Today, forecast.

The next hours, with predicted decision zones from your routine and the mode you pick: work day, day off, sick, vacation.

Improve

Act, then measure.

A zone that helped stays. One that didn't gets replaced. The engine calibrates to your body, not an average one.

Celebrate

Make progress visible.

Streaks, XP and insight-engine levels: the reward that brings people back tomorrow.

05 · Invention

Decision Zones.

The few moments in a day where a small action changes the outcome most, anchored to the routine rather than the clock. What mattered was pre-empting patterns rather than announcing when they'll happen, iterating on decisions until the right method sticks, celebrating improvement, and motivating through failure.

06 · The question engine

Ask only when the answer changes the advice.

Only 30% of users finished the daily reflection. I redesigned it as a one-tap flow built on three rules, and completion went to 66%.

Rule 1

Don't collect what you can infer.

The CGM already says what happened and when. Questions only target the why, and only when it's still ambiguous.

Rule 2

Use the cheapest input that works.

Passive data first, then a one-tap confirm, then check-all, then free entry. "Saw a 6pm workout, right?" beats a blank field.

Rule 3

Ask a delta, not a survey.

"Was yesterday typical?" Yes ends it. No opens a short check-all tied to what changed.

07 · Execution

Three conflicts, three fixes.

Ideas were never the hard part. Getting six people with different jobs to build the same thing was.

Within a role

Web app first, or straight to Swift?

Our two developers disagreed on the stack. Fix: reasoning. We weighed the impact of each path against its effort, and the argument settled itself.

Between roles

More data, or less?

Engineering wanted to capture as much as possible in the cycle; design wanted it dead simple. Fix: priorities and compromise, anchored to the core thesis.

With my co-founder

Habit app, or safety app?

We argued about the logo, about adding developers, and about what Remira should be. Fix: shared vision, and ego out. Pros and cons, then back to impact over effort.

Prove Your Pick · Founder & sole product owner · 2026 to now

A track record nobody can fake.

Prove Your Pick turns stock picking into a social game. Call any stock or ETF, lock it permanently, get judged by the real market, and build a public track record nobody can edit. I wrote the PRD, designed it, and built it end to end as a non-engineer by directing AI coding agents.

01 · The product

Pick, lock, judge, record, share.

1Pick

Make a call on any stock or ETF.

2Lock

Timestamped and permanent. No edits, no deletes.

3Judge

Scored against real market data, not opinions.

4Record

Every call adds to a public, verifiable track record.

5Share

A brag nobody can fake, and an invite to beat it.

02 · The business

Free to play. Pay to follow.

Stock picking has always been gated by credentials and follower counts. Prove Your Pick replaces both with a record anyone can build, and that record is what the business will sell.

  • The hook is a tournament. Locking a pick turns a hot take into a position you're accountable for, and climbing past your friends makes it a game.
  • Every locked pick is verified data. More players means more picks, a sharper crowd signal, and rankings that mean more.
  • At scale, the leaderboard is the product. We'll charge to see the top scorers and to follow the best individuals.
  • It democratizes stock picking. Credibility comes from a public record, not a title, so anyone can earn a following.
  • Sharing is the growth loop. A verified win gets posted; every challenge to a friend is a new player, and the wheel turns again.
03 · The call I'd defend

Integrity before engagement.

I deferred leaderboards, profiles and scoring for the first month. The whole business sells trust in the record, so the record had to be impossible to fudge before anything was built on top of it.

Shape

One codebase, serverless.

A monolith deployed serverless: simplest to build and debug alone, and it scales up before it ever needs to scale out.

Data

SQL, on purpose.

Every pick carries the same fields, picks relate to people and votes, and one action can touch many rows. Structure, relationships, transactions: Postgres.

The trade-off

No cache, for privacy.

Vote on a pick to unlock its discussion. A cached page is built for one person and handed to the next, which here would leak unlocked content. So the product pays in speed to guarantee correctness, knowingly.

05 · Building with AI

AI does what you can't, fast. It doesn't think for you.

01

Learn the system first.

Hours of system design before any code: caching, read vs write load, monolith vs serverless, and how data structures drive cost.

02

Ask precise questions.

Not "make it fast," but where each piece runs. That's how I found the server and database on opposite coasts, adding latency, and moved them together.

03

Never take the first draft.

Double- and triple-check every change, and have a separate model review each file adversarially.

04

Test it like a user.

Click through every feature to confirm the experience matches what the code claims. That catches the most bugs.

Before launch, adversarial threat modeling found four ways to game the record, and all four were closed. The judging logic was mutation-tested five ways, and the pipeline was validated in SQL against production data.
