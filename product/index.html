02 · The process

Double Diamond: diverge, converge, twice.

Discover

Watch the day, not the wishlist.

24 interviews, rebuilt as behavioral walkthroughs of real daily routines instead of feature-wish questions.

Define

Stated wants diverged from behavior.

That killed the most-requested feature, a Google Calendar integration, and re-centered the roadmap on one thesis: daily pull, retention, organic sharing.

Develop

Wireframe, then build the hard part.

Flows for the home screen, reflection and decision zones, plus the glucose visualizations I built myself.

Deliver

Borrow proven loops.

Duolingo-style notifications, Strava-style streaks and Flo-style shared insights between users.

03 · Invention

The Reinforcing Cycle.

Most diabetes apps are dashboards: they show you numbers and leave the work to you. Remira is a loop, and every lap makes it more accurate and more worth opening.

Reflect

Yesterday, wrapped.

Every 24 hours: what happened, one good thing, and whether yesterday's decision zones worked.

Predict

Today, forecast.

The next hours, populated with predicted decision zones from your routine and the modes you pick: work day, day off, sick, vacation.

Improve

Act, then measure.

If a zone helped, it stays. If it didn't, it gets replaced. The engine calibrates to your body, not an average one.

Celebrate

Make progress visible.

Streaks, XP and insight-engine levels: the reward that brings people back tomorrow.

04 · Invention

Decision Zones.

The few moments in a day where a small action changes the outcome most. They're anchored to the routine, not the clock, so "after breakfast" works whether breakfast is at 9 or 11. What you control (doses, carbs, movement) gets a plan. What you can't (sickness, heat, your period) gets a one-tap mode and reassurance, not an interrogation.

1Late-night highAvoid overcorrection lows and late-night snacking.
2Morning highStricter dosing practices in the morning.
3Successful correctionAfternoon exercise stabilizes you. Keep doing it.
4Evening highStricter dosing practices at dinner.
5Night lowLooser dosing before bed.
05 · The question engine

Ask only when the answer changes the advice.

Only 30% of users finished the daily reflection. I redesigned it as a one-tap flow built on a few rules, and completion went to 66%.

Rule 1

Don't collect what you can infer.

The CGM already says what happened and when. Questions only target the why, and only when it's still ambiguous. Most days, that's close to zero questions.

Rule 2

Use the cheapest input that works.

Passive data first, then a one-tap confirm, then check-all, then free entry. "Saw a 6pm workout, right?" beats a blank field.

Rule 3

Ask a delta, not a survey.

One question: "was yesterday typical?" Yes ends it. No opens a short check-all tied to what changed. Friction scales with payoff.

06 · Priorities under scarcity

Six people, one thesis, and a lot of no.

Cut

Forums and sponsored spotlights.

Scored on impact against effort, both lost to the core loop. The roadmap was rebuilt around daily pull, retention and organic sharing.

Killed

The most-requested feature.

A Google Calendar integration was the top request. Walkthroughs of real days showed behavior didn't back it up, so it didn't ship.

Learned

From a competitor teardown.

Multi-user dependency is the biggest growth lever in this category, input friction is the real bottleneck, and the product should stay descriptive, never prescriptive.

07 · Voice as a product surface

Notifications people don't mute.

We wrote the notification system by trigger: inactivity, time of day, streak and XP milestones, and a day-five reverse-psychology sequence. A health app can be warm and funny without being stressful.

01 · The product

Pick, lock, judge, record, share.

1Pick

Make a call on a stock.

2Lock

Timestamped and permanent. No edits, no deletes.

3Judge

Scored against real market data, not opinions.

4Record

Every call adds to a public, verifiable track record.

5Share

A brag nobody can fake, and an invite to beat it.

02 · The hook

Three mechanics that compound.

Tournament

Opinions become a contest.

Locking a pick turns a take into a position you're accountable for. The leaderboard is earned, not claimed, which is what makes winning worth something.

Network effect

The record is the moat.

Every locked pick is a verified, timestamped data point. As players grow, the crowd signal sharpens, and the recommendations and analysis built on it get more accurate, which draws more players.

Social × competition

Sharing is the growth loop.

A verified call is built to be posted, and challenging a friend to beat your record is the cheapest acquisition there is. Competition does the marketing.

03 · The call I'd defend

Integrity before engagement.

I deferred leaderboards, profiles and scoring for the first month. Public rankings are worthless if the record beneath them can be quietly fudged or edited after the fact, so the record had to be trustworthy before anything was built on top of it.

04 · How I built it

Acceptance criteria verified in production, not on my laptop.

Threat model

Attack my own product first.

Adversarial threat modeling found four ways to game the record. All four were closed before launch.

Testing

Break the judge on purpose.

The judging logic was mutation-tested five separate ways, so a subtle scoring bug can't hide behind passing tests.

Data

Check the pipeline in SQL.

Validated the pipeline in SQL against production data, then defined "done" as verified in production.
