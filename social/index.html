The constraint

Every one of those views came from a pool of ten million people.

Type 1 diabetes affects about 10 million people worldwide. Not 10 million in the US. Not 10 million prospects. That is everyone on earth who has it. Most consumer startups fish in a market ten to a hundred times bigger.

The ceiling was the market, not the method. To do 8.7M views in a 10M-person category, the content had to reach nearly everyone it could, plus the parents and partners around them. Point the same system at a normal-sized market and the ceiling moves. The mechanics don't.

The creative

Three formats. Three different jobs.

Most founders pick one and wonder why it stalls. Reach, trust and conversion are different problems. One video can't carry all three.

Viral short-form

Buys the distribution.

Five to seven seconds, text on b-roll, one emotional claim. Cheap to make, fast to test, and the only thing that reaches people who have never heard of you. Six posts cleared 300K. The top one hit 927K off a single line of copy.

The 63K post earned 37× more followers per view than the 927K one. A talking head at 1:41 held 19.2 seconds. A six-second hook held 8.6. You rent reach from the algorithm. Attention is what turns a viewer into an audience.

Method

Why this translates to a company I've never touched.

The obvious objection: it worked because I have Type 1 diabetes. Founder-market fit helped. It wasn't the mechanism, and it was worth less than the ten-million-person ceiling cost me.

Before making anything

I became the target audience on purpose.

I spent weeks training my feed on the niche until the algorithm served me what it serves the customer. Then I built from what I couldn't skip. That's a process, not a biography. It costs two weeks.

What I got wrong

Trends and edits do not sell health.

I assumed polished edits and trending sounds would win. They didn't. Nobody comes to their health looking for something trendy. What worked was disturbing, inspiring or controversial. UGC or faceless barely mattered. Emotion was the variable.

The call I defend hardest

80% of the videos never show the product.

The For You page is where people are most resistant. Pitching there burns the impression. So the content is about the problem the audience shares, not the app. The ad creative lives on the pinned highlights, the store listing and the landing page, where intent already exists.

What I am actually selling

A brand account, not a spike.

A following is name recognition, a community, and a direct line into 5,000 inboxes nobody can outbid you for. The floor between spikes is the number that matters. It went up every month, on both accounts, without a dollar behind it.

The playbook, portable

What I'd do in your first 30 days.

Weeks 1 and 2

  • Train a feed on your category until the algorithm serves me your customer
  • Find what your niche reacts to, not what's trending
  • Stand up the account, brand, bio and pinned product surface
  • Ship 15 posts across all three formats. Read what moves

Weeks 3 and 4

  • Lock the format that worked and scale volume against it
  • Layer UGC and on camera to turn reach into followers
  • Mirror the library to Instagram for free
  • Open direct outreach once the name means something

The run

10M views and 15K followers in one summer, from zero.

End of May to end of August 2025. 100 posts, solo, no face, no budget, no existing audience. Six videos cleared 750K. The best hit 1.5M.

The account became the product. Autopilot and Nummo AI both paid me for brand content, inbound, off the audience alone. That is what a name is worth before you have anything to sell.

Two categories, two runs, same playbook. The second time I built the destination first, then pointed 8.7M views at it.

The method

Read the data, lock the format, scale against it.

01 · Read

Retention over vanity.

I tracked every post's KPIs in Excel and read format performance against audience retention, not likes. The data said what the audience actually stayed for.

02 · Lock

One repeatable format.

Faceless and anonymous, so the edit had to carry everything. Once a format held attention, it became the template rather than a one-off.

03 · Scale

Volume against what works.

100 posts in a summer, all against the proven format. Six cleared 750K, and the audience compounded into 15K followers and two inbound brand deals.

01 · Message before megaphone

The most damaging confusion was "are you replacing my CLI?"

It's the default read of every developer who sees Aether. It ate the first twenty minutes of a call with a senior DevOps engineer and nearly ended it. Once Aether was framed as an augment to the tools they already love, the objection reversed. Every asset now has to pre-empt it in the first ten seconds.

Turn your AI subscription into a dev team.

The landing page went from a feature wall to one proposition. Outbound into developer communities stayed paused until it landed, because you get one credible entry into a developer community, and burning it on a pitch that fails first contact is worse than not entering.

02 · The formats

Show the work. Never pitch the work.

I studied how the developer brands people actually follow (Lovable, Clay) post, then built Aether's own versions of their best formats.

Instagram · TikTok · Shorts

Faceless demos with a caption hook.

Quick screen captures of agents doing real work, with one line of copy that earns the next three seconds. Carousels and on-camera experiments run alongside.

X

Memes, release screenshots, real replies.

Join conversations that already matter, screenshot the releases worth noticing, and build a voice developers recognize. The company account was rebuilt and put on a cadence.

Brand

A bible for how we sound.

Mottos written to the actual fear: "You trust your agents, but do you really?" · "Stop babysitting your agents." · "It loops until it's green."

The format, in practice

“You're reviewing PRs wrong…”

Faceless, one-line hook, built on a screen every developer knows: a pull request that can't merge.

05 · The lift

New users per week, before and during the pilot.

The pilot added as many new users in four weeks as the product had in the previous seven. The week of Aug 24 held above the old average while I was unavailable, which suggests the lift comes from compounding channel presence, not day-to-day posting.

06 · Creators

~50 creators contacted. Three negotiated to contract-ready.

Pipeline

~$9.5K across three creators.

Chosen for audience fit over follower count: each already talks to engineers who run agents, and one placement cross-posts to three platforms.

Negotiation

Price halved, format lengthened.

The lead deal was negotiated to half the ask while upgrading to a full-length review. Cost per thousand views fell from about $667 to $333.

Allocation

Reach per dollar decides.

Hackathon sponsorships were redirected to creator sponsorships on reach per dollar, with replacement creators held in reserve on price, not fit.

07 · Next

10,000 followers across every Aether account in 90 days.

From ~1,300 at the end of the pilot, that's roughly an 8× increase, organic, on a developer tool, with no paid social budget. Most growth hires wouldn't put that number in writing. I did, because Instagram went from zero to a top referrer in five weeks. At current sponsorship rates, ~$9.5K buys a few placements. An owned audience of 10,000 produces comparable reach every month, at no marginal cost.
